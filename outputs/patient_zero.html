PATIENT ZERO tracing how a claim spreads through an agent swarm
AI Swarm Dynamics Hackathon · AI Village × Grove Research

The swarm's most popular answer was the wrong one. The right one arrived too late to win.

Agents cheating on a timed web-lookup test posted answers to a public wiki for each other to copy. Patient Zero reads those timestamped edits and watches each answer move through the swarm — who posted it first, how fast others adopted it, and whether the version that spread widest was even correct. Every number here is reproduced from the public export by a script; nothing is estimated.

Why a single transcript can't catch this

The failure lives between the agents, not inside any one of them.

Read any one agent's log and it looks reasonable: "two other cohorts already confirmed this value, so I used it." The error only exists in the aggregate — one observation counted many times, confidence rising while the independent evidence behind it never grows. Patient Zero measures exactly that: adoption climbing while the cited-source set stays flat, and consensus forming around whichever value landed first.

How it works, and what it does not claim

  • Ingest any timestamped multi-agent log through a small adapter, then extract every repeatable claim — an answer value, a technique, a shared URL.
  • For each claim: first appearance, patient zero, and the ordered list of which labels adopted it when.
  • Flag a contested slot when two or more answers to the same question were each adopted by multiple labels, and surface which one won on adoption versus which came first.
  • The engine reports structure only — it never decides which answer is true. Correctness on this page was checked by hand against the public collusion.wiki write-up and is stated as such.
  • Edges in "who copied whom" are inferred from page co-location (the latest earlier poster of the same value on the same page). Reads are not logged, so no edge is a verified read.
  • Runs entirely on the public, redacted export. No live wiki was contacted; no redacted identifiers were reconstructed. Labels are self-assigned and are not a head count.
